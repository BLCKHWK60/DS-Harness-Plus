# Scout

Plugin `@local/dsh-scout` (dsh 0.2.2-rc.2). A mode and tab, beside Chat, Cowork and Code, for finding work: jobs, freelance projects, contracts and public tenders. It is research, not a tracker: nothing is sent, submitted or applied for.

Off until you switch it on in **Settings → Scout**. Needs the Modes add-on and the Cowork sidebar. Reading PDFs needs the PDF reader add-on.

## What a run does

1. States its understanding: the work, seniority, skills, pay target, work types and markets, and every assumption.
2. Researches the field first: the occupation's names in each language and its skills (ESCO), how many live postings each title variant has in each market (so it searches with the words employers use), pay (from postings, Adzuna's statistics and official pages), and where each work type is found.
3. Plans: picks sources from the registry and writes the queries per source and language. `scout_sources` names each market and work type that no ready source covers.
4. Searches, reads the promising postings, removes duplicates, then scores each opportunity on fit, freshness, pay and legitimacy from 1 to 5, each with a written reason, and flags likely ghost jobs and scams with the signals behind each flag. Warning signs found in a posting's text by patterns are signs to verify, not verdicts, and do not lower the legitimacy Scout suggests: text checks are hints to verify; the score and its reason are the model's judgement (see "Scores and warning signs").
5. Plans the approach for the best ones: who to contact (a role; a name only if a source shows it), which channel, when, and a short draft based on a real trigger in the posting or the news. Drafts only: nothing is sent.
6. Compares with the last report (what is new since that run, what it ranked, what it found but did not rank, what is gone), writes the report and saves it in the Scout folder as Markdown and a table (CSV).

Not built yet: saved search recipes and learning from your feedback (planned step 4), and Source Discovery with its review list (planned step 5).

## The start page

Work, place, pay target, work type, and the markets as chips (USA, UK, Germany and Spain to begin with; add or remove any). **+ Files** puts a resume, LinkedIn export (PDF) or notes into the Scout folder. What was selected when a conversation started stays that conversation's selection.

## Your files and your profile

- **Files** stay in the Scout folder (`scout/files` in the app's home unless you set `folder`). Scout reads them with whichever model the conversation uses, local or cloud (since 0.51.2; only Recon keeps cloud models out).
- **The profile** is a short summary: titles, years, skills, languages, pay and what you want. Write it in Settings → Scout, or ask Scout to write it from your files; Scout is told to show you the draft and save it only after you agree (the tool itself does not ask). Each save keeps the profile before it as `profile.md.bak` next to it.
- `/scout` shows which model the conversation uses and how many reports are saved.

## Contact details never go into a search

Scout is told to search by titles, skills and places, never your name or contact details. A guard holds to the part that can be checked: in a Scout conversation and the helpers it starts, any call that goes out (Scout's own searches, `web_search`, `web_fetch`, `deep_research`, Finance's tools) is refused when its arguments hold an email address or a phone number. The refusal shows the address or number only in part. Reading your own files, the profile and the reports is not a search and is not checked. Dates and date ranges (`01.10.2026-31.03.2027`), pay ranges, also written without spaces (`70.000-90.000`, `45.000-55.000€`) or with a space between the thousands (`45 000-55 000 €`, `1 000 000`: only that shape, a group of exactly three digits from 0 after a short group, then only more groups of exactly three digits, up to a dash, a currency or the end of the number, so `Room 101 0044 20 7946 0958`, `Musterweg 3 0151 2345 6789`, `Mo-Fr 9 07700 900123`, `Office 2 030 123 456 78` and `Zimmer 12 030 123 45 67` are refused), codes (ISCO, CPV, NAICS) and the reference numbers in a posting's address are not taken for phone numbers. Dotted or comma groups right after an international prefix or a trunk group from 0 are the number's own, never an amount (`+34 612.345.678`, `0034 612.345.678`, `+49 151 234.567`, `07700 900.123`); a 5-digit postcode from 0 just before a pay range (`01067 45.000-55.000`) reads the same way, so such a search is refused. A `+N` just before a pay range, with dots, commas or spaces between the thousands, is a time zone or the like, not a phone prefix (`Remote GMT +1 70.000-90.000`, `remote (UTC +1) 70.000-90.000 EUR`, `Remote CET +1 45 000-55 000 €`). Spanish numbers (`612 345 678`, `612 34 56 78`, `612.345.678`, `612-345-678`; with dots not when round, `650.000.000`, or next to a currency) count, and so do bare runs of 9 or 10 digits in free text (`612345678`, `2025550100`), but not in a web address, where they are usually ids. A program's own requests under the app's PTC preset are not checked by this guard; the tools a program calls are.

## Scratch

A Scratch conversation (the Cowork sidebar's private place) works as any other: its reports are saved, listed and compared for what is new. When it is deleted for good, Scout removes the reports it saved in Scout's own folder (never in a folder you picked yourself), its postings and its saved selection.

## Sources

A registry you control in Settings → Scout: switch any source on or off, hide a starting one, add your own. Each source records how it may be reached, and Scout holds to it:

| Access | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Interface | Searched directly with `scout_search`. |
| Single pages | Pages may be read one at a time with `web_fetch`. |
| Search engine only | Found through web search; its pages are never fetched. A guard refuses `web_fetch` on these sites in Scout conversations, including their country sites (uk.indeed.com, stepstone.at). |

Starting sources with an interface: Bundesagentur für Arbeit (Germany, no key), Arbeitnow (Germany and remote, no key), Hacker News hiring threads (no key), Greenhouse, Lever and Ashby company boards (no key; give the company's board name), Adzuna (13 markets, free key `APP_ID:APP_KEY`), USAJOBS (free key `EMAIL:KEY`), Reed (UK, free key), and the public tenders below. Keys are kept in the app's secret store.

**Public tenders, US and UK** (work type Public tender, and Freelance):

| Source | Market | Key | How it is read |
|---|---|---|---|
| Contracts Finder | UK | none (open data) | Its OCDS search (`/Published/Notices/OCDS/Search`, GET). It has no word search that answers to GET, so Scout reads the newest tender notices of the last 10 days (`days` up to 30; at most 5 pages of 100), keeps the latest version of each, drops closed and cancelled ones, and keeps those that match your words in the title, description and lots. |
| Find a Tender | UK | none (open data) | Its OCDS release packages (`/api/1.0/ocdsReleasePackages`, GET), read the same way. |
| SAM.gov contract opportunities | USA | free key from your SAM.gov account | The Get Opportunities API v2: solicitations, combined synopses, presolicitations and sources sought (work still open to bids), searched by title, with a two-letter state as the place. SAM.gov takes its key only in the address; an error names the service only, and the key is never kept with a posting. A personal key allows only a few requests a day. |

A tender shows its contract value (never counted as pay), its closing date and the buyer's published contact.

Also listed: GOV.UK Find a job, EURES, TED, IT Jobs Watch, ESCO and O*NET (single pages); Indeed, LinkedIn, StepStone, freelancermap, GULP, Totaljobs, Jobserve, Dice, Wellfound, InfoJobs, Tecnoempleo and Malt, and the freelance boards Upwork, PeoplePerHour, YunoJuno, Freelancer.com and Guru (search engine only: never fetched, never signed in to).

## What the assistant is given

- The method (about 3,560 characters, under the Modes limit of 4,000) and the start-page selection, with how many files are in the Scout folder, whether a profile is saved and how many earlier reports there are (helpers get the selection only). Scout's mode carries `keepPrompt: true`, so its method and rules stay even when a model version is set to stand alone in Settings → Model Tiers (the Modes add-on honours this from 0.57.0).
- These tools, and no others: `scout_sources`, `scout_search`, `scout_posting`, `scout_titles`, `scout_demand`, `scout_pay`, `scout_assess`, `scout_files`, `scout_profile`, `scout_report`, `scout_reports`; `web_search` and `web_fetch`, `deep_research` (Scout always has the web: its chat box shows a fixed "Web · Always on" label, Off only while Tools are off), Finance's news, filings and figures tools when Finance is on, `diagram_draw`, `todo_write`, `ask_user_question`. No file or shell tools. Under the app's PTC preset the model calls these tools from programs (`run_code`), which the app offers in every mode.
- Scout's own tools exist only in Scout conversations; other modes do not see them. Helpers a Scout conversation starts (Deep research) get the search, research and checking tools, but never `scout_files`, `scout_profile`, `scout_report` or `scout_reports`: they read web pages that may carry hidden instructions.

## Scores and warning signs (step 3)

`scout_assess` gives Scout's own checks on postings found in the conversation, as suggested scores with their reasons. The model scores fit itself, and may differ from a suggestion if it says why.

| Score | What Scout computes |
|---|---|
| Freshness | Age: 5 up to 7 days, 4 up to 14, 3 up to 30, 2 up to 60, then 1; one less when the job was posted again (first posted 21 or more days earlier). A tender is judged by the days left to respond. Closed: 1. |
| Pay | The top of the posting's range against your pay target (start page, or a target the model passes, for example from your profile), in the same currency and period: 5 at or above, 4 within 10%, 3 within 25%, 2 within 50%, else 1. No pay given: 3. Another currency or period: not compared. |
| Legitimacy | From computed facts only: 5, one less for each computed ghost-job sign (still listed after 45 days, posted again, a text under 400 characters; at least 2); 2 with one computed scam sign (pay far above a typical pay from a source), 1 with two or more. No pay is shown but does not lower it. Warning signs found in the text never lower it, however many: they are listed for the model to judge against the whole posting. Things to check and notes do not lower it either. The reason names computed signs ("1 ghost-job sign", or "no computed warning signs") and then the text ones ("1 warning sign found in the text, not counted: judge it against the whole posting and the words quoted"). This is a suggestion: text checks are hints to verify; the score and its reason are the model's judgement. |

Warning signs, each shown with the words behind it where there are some. Two sources, shown differently:

- Computed facts, stated as they are, and the only ones that lower the suggested legitimacy: the posting's age, a repost, no pay (shown, not counted), pay against a typical pay or the other postings found, a text under 400 characters (`ghost sign: no pay given`, `scam sign: pay far above the market: …`).
- Signs found by text patterns (a fee, a request for documents or bank details, promised earnings, a personal address or a messaging app as the only contact, talent-pool wording): wording can mislead either way, so each is a sign to verify, never a verdict, shown with its reason and the words behind it: `warning sign (check): asks the applicant for money — 'pay a registration fee'`. They do not lower the suggested legitimacy: the model judges them against the whole posting. `scout_posting`, `scout_assess` and the saved report all say so.

What is checked:

- Ghost jobs: still listed more than 45 days after posting; posted again (the source gives a first posting date 21 or more days earlier, or the same job, by title, company and town, is found again with posting dates 21 or more days apart); no pay given (shown, but it does not lower legitimacy); talent-pool or "future opportunities" wording (a text sign, not counted); a full text under 400 characters.
- Scams: asking the applicant for money (by name: registration, onboarding, starter-kit and background-check fees, an advance fee, "you must pay a fee or deposit", and `you`, `you'll`, `you will`, `candidates will` or `applicants will` pay with an article and up to two words before fee or deposit: "you will pay a one-time processing fee", "you'll need to pay a refundable deposit", "candidates will pay an administration fee"; a starter kit to buy; only when the text asks them of the applicant and the applicant is the payer: application, processing, administration and placement fees, advance payments, refundable deposits, gift cards, training fees, a bare German `Gebühr` or `Kaution`, transfers or cheques, which finance and payroll work and agencies also name); asking for personal documents or bank details (passport or ID copies, social security number in any case, bank or card details), only when asked of the applicant and only the applicant's own (`your`, `Ihre`, `deine`, `tus`, `sus` a few words before), sent to someone named (`us`, `me`, `the recruiter`, `uns`, `nos`) or asked for with a "please" (`bitte senden Sie`); contact only through a personal email address (gmail.com and the like) or a messaging app; promised earnings rather than pay; pay far above the market (1.75 times a typical pay the model read in a source for that market; a computed sign). English, German and Spanish wording; a "never", "no", "not", "won't", "waive" or "free of" before the words, in their clause and up to 40 characters back, turns them around ("We never ask for a registration fee"), and so do "is waived", "is not required" and "paid by our client" just after; the employer or the agency's client paying ("we cover", "our client pays", "unser Kunde übernimmt", "der Arbeitgeber übernimmt", "la empresa paga", "el cliente paga") is not a request.
- "Asked of the applicant" means a request near the words ("send", "please send", "kindly send", "provide your", "you must", "you'll need to", "applicants must pay", "is required", "senden Sie", the German du-forms "schick", "überweise", "zahle", "envía", and Spanish with the pronoun attached, "envíanos", "envíenos", "mándanos"), and no "reimbursement", "after your offer" or "first day" after them. "You will <verb>" describes the work and is not a request. So "a platform for gift cards", "credit card information security", "your bank details for direct deposit after your offer is signed", "you will upload supplier bank details" and "- Enter employee bank details into the payroll system" are not signs; "You'll need to buy gift cards", "Kindly pay the processing fee of $50 to the recruiter" and "Kindly send a copy of your passport" are.
- "The applicant is the payer" (application, processing, administration, placement fees and the like) means `you`, `you'll`, `your`, `applicants` or `candidates` near the verb ("you must pay", "pay your", "candidates are asked to wire"), a recipient right after it ("pay us") or just after the fee ("to the recruiter"), a "please" or "kindly", or a German or Spanish request ("zahlen Sie", "überweise", "paga", "debes pagar"). A bare bullet or an imperative after a colon reads the same as a list of duties and is not enough: "Responsibilities: - Pay supplier invoices and processing fees" and "Your tasks: - Pay placement fees to agencies on time" are not signs, nor is "pay attention to processing fees" ("pay attention", "pay close attention" is never a payment) or "the deposit for new tenants".
- A recipient (`us`, `me`, `uns`, `nos`, the recruiter) counts only as the object of the request: right after its verb ("send us", "senden Sie uns", "schick uns", "envíanos") or "to us", "to me", "to the recruiter" just after the details. "Join us to manage payroll: provide bank details of new hires to the payroll provider" is not a sign.
- A "please" ("kindly", "bitte", "por favor") or a recipient ("send us", "envíanos") counts only for the money or document words it asks for, not for any such words in the sentence: when an "if", "when", "who", "wenn", "falls", "si", "cuando" or the like, or "experience", "Erfahrung", "experiencia", stands between the request and the words, the request is for something else. "Send us your CV if you have experience with placement fees and invoicing", "Please send your CV if you have experience with bank details and payroll", "Bitte senden Sie uns Ihren Lebenslauf, wenn Sie Erfahrung mit Bankdaten haben" and "Envíanos tu CV si tienes experiencia con datos bancarios de clientes" are not signs; "If you are shortlisted, please send your bank details" is.
- A "never", "free of charge" or "we cover" turns words around only within their clause. A clause ends at `;`, `:`, "but", "except", "aber", "pero", "sino" and the like, and at a comma that starts a new clause. A comma in a list does not: after a list's item ("registration fees, onboarding fees"), or with "or", "and", "nor", "oder", "und", "ni", "y" or "o" in the list ("We do not charge application, processing or registration fees", "We never ask for money, bank details, or a registration fee", "We cover travel, accommodation, and the registration fee", "Nunca te pediremos dinero, una cuota de inscripción ni tus datos bancarios" are not signs), unless "you", "we" and the like start a clause after the comma or a verb makes the list a clause of its own. So "Training is free of charge, (and) you only pay a registration fee", "The interview is free of charge but a registration fee of $50 is required" and "The interview is free of charge, a registration fee and a deposit are required" are signs; "We cover the training fee, the registration fee and the deposit" is not. The checks after the words ("is waived", "after your offer", "is required") stop at the same breaks, and a comma right before "or", "and" and the like keeps a list there too ("The registration fee, travel, and accommodation are waived").
- Documents also include a passport or ID sent on request ("send us your passport", "Mándanos tu pasaporte", `Reisepass`, `Personalausweis`, `DNI`, `NIE`) and the German `Bankdaten` and `Kontonummer`.
- In a tender, fees are often part of the work (the supplier collects them, the contract covers them): the money check counts only money the bidder is to pay: `you`, `you'll`, `bidders`, `tenderers` or `suppliers` with `must`, `need to`, `have to`, `are required to` or `will need to` and `pay`; "payable by bidders"; "please pay … before you submit" or "… with your bid". Shown as "asks the bidder for money". "Residents must pay a permit fee; the supplier will collect it" is not a sign, nor is "the supplier shall pay the registration fee" (`shall` states the contract's terms, so it is not on the list) or "bidders must pay attention to the administration fee".
- Things to check (shown, never counted): pay 1.75 times the median of at least 5 other postings found in the same currency and period (they mix markets, titles and levels, so this is not a scam sign: the model is asked to compare with a typical pay from `scout_pay`); on Hacker News, contact only through a personal email address (founders often post from their own).
- Notes: the same title from the same company in three or more towns (one role offered in several places is common).
- A tender's published contact is the buyer's, so its contact address is not checked, by `scout_posting` or `scout_assess`.
- The typical pay the model gives `scout_assess` (`typical_pay`) is used only with `ids`, the postings of that market, and is kept with those postings, so the saved report's `signals` say the same as `scout_assess` did. Without `ids` (the newest 40 may mix markets) it is neither used nor kept, and the answer asks for that market's ids.

The text checks run when a posting is read with `scout_posting` (the result is kept with the posting) or on the text a source gives with its search results. Adzuna gives only a short extract, and the checks say so.

## Reports

- `scout_report` saves the report as Markdown and a CSV table. Each row carries the posting number, the four scores, each with its reason (`fit_reason`, `freshness_reason`, `pay_reason`, `legitimacy_reason`), flags, and for the top ones `contact`, `channel`, `timing`, `trigger` and `draft`. The save is refused, with the list of what to fix, while a score is missing or outside 1 to 5, a score has no reason, a draft has no trigger, or a draft is over 1,000 characters.
- Scout itself fills two columns, never the model: `signals` (its own checks on the posting: computed facts as they are, and `warning sign (check): … — '…'` for text checks to verify on the posting's page, which are not verdicts and are not counted in Scout's suggested legitimacy; `none found (text not read)` when the posting's text was never read, and `; text not read` after the signs then; the "Checked by Scout" section says the same above its list) and `new` (`new`; `seen <date>` when an earlier run ranked the same job; `seen <date>, not ranked` when an earlier run found it but did not rank it; the same job by address, or by title, company and town). It also fills empty title, company, place, pay, date and link from the posting, and adds a "Checked by Scout" section at the end of the Markdown. The Markdown says that messages are drafts and nothing was sent.
- Beside each report, `<name>.seen.json` keeps the keys (address; title, company and town) of every posting the run found, ranked or not, never their text (at most 3,000 keys; written whole or not at all). That is what "new" is measured against. Reports saved before 0.57.0 have no such file: for them the comparison says "not in that report" and warns that the run may have seen more than it ranked.
- Reports a conversation saved itself are never its "earlier reports": saving again after a change does not mark every row as seen. A second save in the same minute with the same title gets `-2` in its name instead of overwriting the first.
- `scout_reports` lists the saved reports (newest first, by the time in their name; files without one, such as your own notes, last), reads one (Markdown, or its table with `.csv`), and compares this conversation's postings with one report (default the newest saved by another conversation): new since then, ranked then too (with the earlier rank), found then but not ranked, and in that report but not found again.
- Settings → Scout → Saved reports lists the newest 20, with **Show reports in Finder**.

## Research tools (step 2)

| Tool | What it gives | Source |
|---|---|---|
| `scout_titles` | The occupation's official and common names in each language of the selected markets, its ISCO group, its essential and optional skills. | ESCO, the EU's list of occupations and skills (public, no key). Its names are official ones; they are checked against live postings. |

## Limits

- Each conversation may make 400 requests to the sources, counted across restarts. A Contracts Finder or Find a Tender search counts once but may read up to 5 pages.
- Each conversation's postings, their numbers (P1, P2, ...), the checks on their text, the other posting dates of a job found again, the typical pay given for them, the names of the reports it saved and its request count are kept in `scout/postings` in the app's home, so a conversation reopened after a restart goes on with the same numbers. The newest 200 conversations are kept; a deleted conversation's file stays until it falls out of those 200.
- The warning signs are hints from wording and numbers, not proof: those found by text patterns are labelled as signs to verify and do not lower the suggested legitimacy; text checks are hints to verify; the score and its reason are the model's judgement. A legitimate posting can trip one, and a scam can avoid all of them. Results depend on what each source lists and on the model. Check a posting on its own page before acting on it.
- Adzuna, USAJOBS, Reed and SAM.gov were built from their documentation and tested with stand-in answers; they are untested against the real services until a key is added. SAM.gov's full description is read from the `noticedesc` address its answers give; that part is untested too. Scout asks SAM.gov for four notice types by sending `ptype` four times (`o`, `k`, `p`, `r`); its documentation shows `ptype` with one value only, so whether the service reads all four is untested: if it reads only the first, searches return solicitations only.
